## 6(a)

Solve $x-5+\frac{4}{x}=0$.

- A. $x=-1\text{ or }x=-4$
- B. $x=1\text{ or }x=4$
- C. $x=0\text{ or }x=4$
- D. $x=1\text{ only}$

Correct answer: B. $x=1\text{ or }x=4$

Worked solution (based on the supplied answer key):

The original denominator requires $x\neq0$. Multiply the equation by $x$:

$$x^2-5x+4=0.$$

Factorise: $(x-1)(x-4)=0$.

Therefore $x=1$ or $x=4$. Both satisfy the original equation and its denominator restriction.

Source: C1 Algebra, Worksheet E, Exercise 6(a); C1 Algebra - Questions.pdf, PDF page 8; C1 Algebra - Answers.pdf, PDF page 13 (Worksheet E answers, page 3).

## 6(b)

Solve $x-\frac{10}{x}=3$.

- A. $x=2\text{ or }x=-5$
- B. $x=-2\text{ or }x=-5$
- C. $x=0\text{ or }x=5$
- D. $x=-2\text{ or }x=5$

Correct answer: D. $x=-2\text{ or }x=5$

Worked solution (based on the supplied answer key):

The original denominator requires $x\neq0$. Multiply by $x$:

$$x^2-10=3x\quad\Rightarrow\quad x^2-3x-10=0.$$

Factorise: $(x+2)(x-5)=0$.

Therefore $x=-2$ or $x=5$. Both are valid in the original equation.

Source: C1 Algebra, Worksheet E, Exercise 6(b); C1 Algebra - Questions.pdf, PDF page 8; C1 Algebra - Answers.pdf, PDF page 13 (Worksheet E answers, page 3).

## 6(c)

Solve $2x^3-x^2-3x=0$.

- A. $x=-1,\;0\text{ or }\frac{3}{2}$
- B. $x=-1\text{ or }x=\frac{3}{2}$
- C. $x=-\frac{3}{2},\;0\text{ or }1$
- D. $x=-1,\;0\text{ or }3$

Correct answer: A. $x=-1,\;0\text{ or }\frac{3}{2}$

Worked solution (based on the supplied answer key):

Take out the common factor $x$, rather than dividing by $x$ and losing the zero solution:

$$x(2x^2-x-3)=0.$$

Factorise the quadratic: $$x(2x-3)(x+1)=0.$$

Therefore $x=0$, $x=\frac{3}{2}$ or $x=-1$. All three satisfy the original equation.

Source: C1 Algebra, Worksheet E, Exercise 6(c); C1 Algebra - Questions.pdf, PDF page 8; C1 Algebra - Answers.pdf, PDF page 13 (Worksheet E answers, page 3).

## 6(d)

Solve $x^2(10-x^2)=9$.

- A. $x=1\text{ or }x=3$
- B. $x=\pm1\text{ only}$
- C. $x=\pm1\text{ or }x=\pm3$
- D. $x=\pm3\text{ only}$

Correct answer: C. $x=\pm1\text{ or }x=\pm3$

Worked solution (based on the supplied answer key):

Expand and rearrange:

$$10x^2-x^4=9\quad\Rightarrow\quad x^4-10x^2+9=0.$$

Factorise as a quadratic in $x^2$: $$(x^2-1)(x^2-9)=0.$$

Thus $x^2=1$ or $x^2=9$. Taking both positive and negative square roots gives $x=\pm1$ or $x=\pm3$.

Source: C1 Algebra, Worksheet E, Exercise 6(d); C1 Algebra - Questions.pdf, PDF page 8; C1 Algebra - Answers.pdf, PDF page 13 (Worksheet E answers, page 3).

## 6(e)

Solve $\frac{5}{x^2}+\frac{4}{x}-1=0$.

- A. $x=1\text{ or }x=-5$
- B. $x=-1\text{ or }x=-5$
- C. $x=-1\text{ or }x=5$
- D. $x=0\text{ or }x=5$

Correct answer: C. $x=-1\text{ or }x=5$

Worked solution (based on the supplied answer key):

The original denominators require $x\neq0$. Multiply by $x^2$:

$$5+4x-x^2=0\quad\Rightarrow\quad x^2-4x-5=0.$$

Factorise: $(x+1)(x-5)=0$.

Therefore $x=-1$ or $x=5$. Neither is excluded by the original denominators, and both satisfy the original equation.

Source: C1 Algebra, Worksheet E, Exercise 6(e); C1 Algebra - Questions.pdf, PDF page 8; C1 Algebra - Answers.pdf, PDF page 13 (Worksheet E answers, page 3).

## 6(f)

Solve $\frac{x-6}{x-4}=x$.

- A. $x=-2\text{ or }x=-3$
- B. $x=2\text{ or }x=3$
- C. $x=3\text{ or }x=4$
- D. $x=2\text{ only}$

Correct answer: B. $x=2\text{ or }x=3$

Worked solution (based on the supplied answer key):

The original denominator requires $x\neq4$. Multiply by $x-4$:

$$x-6=x(x-4)=x^2-4x.$$

Rearrange and factorise: $$x^2-5x+6=0\quad\Rightarrow\quad(x-2)(x-3)=0.$$

Therefore $x=2$ or $x=3$. Both satisfy the original equation and neither equals $4$.

Source: C1 Algebra, Worksheet E, Exercise 6(f); C1 Algebra - Questions.pdf, PDF page 8; C1 Algebra - Answers.pdf, PDF page 13 (Worksheet E answers, page 3).

## 6(g)

Solve $x+5=\frac{3}{x+3}$.

- A. $x=6\text{ or }x=2$
- B. $x=-3\text{ or }x=-2$
- C. $x=-6\text{ only}$
- D. $x=-6\text{ or }x=-2$

Correct answer: D. $x=-6\text{ or }x=-2$

Worked solution (based on the supplied answer key):

The original denominator requires $x\neq-3$. Multiply by $x+3$:

$$(x+5)(x+3)=3.$$

Expand and rearrange: $$x^2+8x+15=3\quad\Rightarrow\quad x^2+8x+12=0.$$

Factorise: $(x+6)(x+2)=0$. Therefore $x=-6$ or $x=-2$. Both are valid in the original equation.

Source: C1 Algebra, Worksheet E, Exercise 6(g); C1 Algebra - Questions.pdf, PDF page 8; C1 Algebra - Answers.pdf, PDF page 13 (Worksheet E answers, page 3).

## 6(h)

Solve $x^2-\frac{4}{x^2}=3$.

- A. $x=\pm2$
- B. $x=2\text{ only}$
- C. $x=\pm1\text{ or }x=\pm2$
- D. $x=\pm4$

Correct answer: A. $x=\pm2$

Worked solution (based on the supplied answer key):

The original denominator requires $x\neq0$. Multiply by $x^2$:

$$x^4-4=3x^2\quad\Rightarrow\quad x^4-3x^2-4=0.$$

Factorise: $$(x^2+1)(x^2-4)=0.$$

The equation $x^2=-1$ has no real solutions. From $x^2=4$, $x=\pm2$. Both satisfy the original equation and its denominator restriction.

Source: C1 Algebra, Worksheet E, Exercise 6(h); C1 Algebra - Questions.pdf, PDF page 8; C1 Algebra - Answers.pdf, PDF page 13 (Worksheet E answers, page 3).

## 6(i)

Solve $4x^4+7x^2=2$.

- A. $x=\pm\frac{1}{4}$
- B. $x=\frac{1}{2}\text{ only}$
- C. $x=\pm\frac{1}{2}$
- D. $x=\pm2$

Correct answer: C. $x=\pm\frac{1}{2}$

Worked solution (based on the supplied answer key):

Rearrange: $$4x^4+7x^2-2=0.$$

Factorise as a quadratic in $x^2$: $$(4x^2-1)(x^2+2)=0.$$

Thus $x^2=\frac{1}{4}$ or $x^2=-2$. The latter has no real solutions.

Taking both square roots of $\frac{1}{4}$ gives $x=\pm\frac{1}{2}$.

Source: C1 Algebra, Worksheet E, Exercise 6(i); C1 Algebra - Questions.pdf, PDF page 8; C1 Algebra - Answers.pdf, PDF page 13 (Worksheet E answers, page 3).

## 6(j)

Solve $\frac{2x}{3-x}=\frac{1}{x+2}$.

- A. $x=3\text{ or }x=-\frac{1}{2}$
- B. $x=-3\text{ or }x=\frac{1}{2}$
- C. $x=-2\text{ or }x=\frac{1}{2}$
- D. $x=-3\text{ or }x=-\frac{1}{2}$

Correct answer: B. $x=-3\text{ or }x=\frac{1}{2}$

Worked solution (based on the supplied answer key):

The original denominators require $x\neq3$ and $x\neq-2$. Multiply by $(3-x)(x+2)$:

$$2x(x+2)=3-x.$$

Expand and rearrange: $$2x^2+4x=3-x\quad\Rightarrow\quad2x^2+5x-3=0.$$

Factorise: $(2x-1)(x+3)=0$. Therefore $x=\frac{1}{2}$ or $x=-3$. Both satisfy the original equation and neither is an excluded value.

Source: C1 Algebra, Worksheet E, Exercise 6(j); C1 Algebra - Questions.pdf, PDF page 8; C1 Algebra - Answers.pdf, PDF page 13 (Worksheet E answers, page 3).

## 6(k)

Solve $\frac{2x+1}{x+3}=\frac{2}{x}$.

- A. $x=-\frac{3}{2}\text{ or }x=2$
- B. $x=\frac{3}{2}\text{ or }x=-2$
- C. $x=-3\text{ or }x=2$
- D. $x=0\text{ or }x=2$

Correct answer: A. $x=-\frac{3}{2}\text{ or }x=2$

Worked solution (based on the supplied answer key):

The original denominators require $x\neq-3$ and $x\neq0$. Multiply by $x(x+3)$:

$$x(2x+1)=2(x+3).$$

Expand and rearrange: $$2x^2+x=2x+6\quad\Rightarrow\quad2x^2-x-6=0.$$

Factorise: $(2x+3)(x-2)=0$. Therefore $x=-\frac{3}{2}$ or $x=2$. Both satisfy the original equation and its denominator restrictions.

Source: C1 Algebra, Worksheet E, Exercise 6(k); C1 Algebra - Questions.pdf, PDF page 8; C1 Algebra - Answers.pdf, PDF page 13 (Worksheet E answers, page 3).

## 6(l)

Solve $\frac{7}{x+2}-3x=2$.

- A. $x=3\text{ or }x=-\frac{1}{3}$
- B. $x=-2\text{ or }x=\frac{1}{3}$
- C. $x=-3\text{ or }x=-\frac{1}{3}$
- D. $x=-3\text{ or }x=\frac{1}{3}$

Correct answer: D. $x=-3\text{ or }x=\frac{1}{3}$

Worked solution (based on the supplied answer key):

The original denominator requires $x\neq-2$. Multiply by $x+2$:

$$7-3x(x+2)=2(x+2).$$

Expand and rearrange: $$7-3x^2-6x=2x+4\quad\Rightarrow\quad3x^2+8x-3=0.$$

Factorise: $(3x-1)(x+3)=0$. Therefore $x=\frac{1}{3}$ or $x=-3$. Both satisfy the original equation and neither equals $-2$.

Source: C1 Algebra, Worksheet E, Exercise 6(l); C1 Algebra - Questions.pdf, PDF page 8; C1 Algebra - Answers.pdf, PDF page 13 (Worksheet E answers, page 3).
