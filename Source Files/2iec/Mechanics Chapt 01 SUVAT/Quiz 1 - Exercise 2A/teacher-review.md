## 2A Q1

A particle moves in a straight line with constant acceleration $3\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$. At time $t=0$ its speed is $2\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$. Find its speed at $6\,\mathrm{s}$.

- A. $18\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$
- B. $20\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$
- C. $8\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$
- D. $36\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$

Correct answer: B. $20\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$

Worked solution:

Use $v=u+at$ with $u=2$, $a=3$ and $t=6$.

$$v=2+3(6)=20\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}.$$

## 2A Q3

A car approaching traffic lights is travelling at $10\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$. Braking brings it to rest with constant deceleration in $16\,\mathrm{s}$. Modelling the car as a particle, find its deceleration.

- A. $-0.625\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$
- B. $1.6\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$
- C. $6.25\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$
- D. $0.625\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$

Correct answer: D. $0.625\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$

Worked solution:

Take the initial direction of motion as positive. From $v=u+at$,

$$0=10+16a,\qquad a=-0.625\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}.$$

Deceleration is the positive magnitude: $0.625\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$.

Source: M1, Exercise 2A, Question 3; Source Files/2iec/Mechanics Chapt 01 SUVAT/M1 chapter 1 exercises.pdf, PDF page 9; Source Files/2iec/Mechanics Chapt 01 SUVAT/Solutions chapter 01.pdf, PDF page 3 (Exercise A, Question 3).

## 2A Q5

A car accelerates uniformly along a straight road between signposts $360\,\mathrm{m}$ apart. It takes $15\,\mathrm{s}$ to travel between them and passes the second at $28\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$. Find its speed at the first signpost.

- A. $20\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$
- B. $24\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$
- C. $48\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$
- D. $52\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$

Correct answer: A. $20\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$

Worked solution:

Use $s=\frac{u+v}{2}t$.

$$360=\frac{u+28}{2}(15).$$

Thus $u+28=48$, so the initial speed is $20\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$.

Source: M1, Exercise 2A, Question 5; Source Files/2iec/Mechanics Chapt 01 SUVAT/M1 chapter 1 exercises.pdf, PDF page 9; Source Files/2iec/Mechanics Chapt 01 SUVAT/Solutions chapter 01.pdf, PDF page 5 (Exercise A, Question 5).

## 2A Q7

A cyclist travels along a straight road from A to B with constant acceleration $0.5\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$. Her speed at A is $3\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$ and the journey takes $12\,\mathrm{s}$. Find (a) her speed at B and (b) the distance AB.

- A. a) $6\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$; b) $54\,\mathrm{m}$
- B. a) $9\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$; b) $108\,\mathrm{m}$
- C. a) $9\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$; b) $72\,\mathrm{m}$
- D. a) $9\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$; b) $36\,\mathrm{m}$

Correct answer: C. a) $9\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$; b) $72\,\mathrm{m}$

Worked solution:

(a) $v=u+at=3+0.5(12)=9\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$.

(b) Use the mean velocity:

$$s=\frac{3+9}{2}(12)=72\,\mathrm{m}.$$

Source: M1, Exercise 2A, Question 7; Source Files/2iec/Mechanics Chapt 01 SUVAT/M1 chapter 1 exercises.pdf, PDF page 9; Source Files/2iec/Mechanics Chapt 01 SUVAT/Solutions chapter 01.pdf, PDF page 7 (Exercise A, Question 7).

## 2A Q9

A particle moves from A to B in a straight line with constant deceleration $1.2\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$. The journey takes $6\,\mathrm{s}$; its speed at B is $2\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$ and its direction has not changed. Find (a) its speed at A and (b) the distance AB.

- A. a) $5.2\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$; b) $21.6\,\mathrm{m}$
- B. a) $9.2\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$; b) $33.6\,\mathrm{m}$
- C. a) $9.2\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$; b) $55.2\,\mathrm{m}$
- D. a) $7.2\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$; b) $27.6\,\mathrm{m}$

Correct answer: B. a) $9.2\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$; b) $33.6\,\mathrm{m}$

Worked solution:

Take A to B as positive, so $a=-1.2\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$.

(a) $2=u-1.2(6)$, giving $u=9.2\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$.

(b) $$s=\frac{9.2+2}{2}(6)=33.6\,\mathrm{m}.$$

Source: M1, Exercise 2A, Question 9; Source Files/2iec/Mechanics Chapt 01 SUVAT/M1 chapter 1 exercises.pdf, PDF page 9; Source Files/2iec/Mechanics Chapt 01 SUVAT/Solutions chapter 01.pdf, PDF page 9 (Exercise A, Question 9).

## 2A Q10

A train slows down on a straight track with constant deceleration $0.6\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$. It passes a signal at $72\,\mathrm{km\,h^{-1}}$ and a second signal $25\,\mathrm{s}$ later. Find (a) its speed at the second signal in km/h and (b) the distance between the signals.

- A. a) $18\,\mathrm{km\,h^{-1}}$; b) $312.5\,\mathrm{m}$
- B. a) $5\,\mathrm{km\,h^{-1}}$; b) $312.5\,\mathrm{m}$
- C. a) $57\,\mathrm{km\,h^{-1}}$; b) $1612.5\,\mathrm{m}$
- D. a) $18\,\mathrm{km\,h^{-1}}$; b) $125\,\mathrm{m}$

Correct answer: A. a) $18\,\mathrm{km\,h^{-1}}$; b) $312.5\,\mathrm{m}$

Worked solution:

Convert first: $72\,\mathrm{km\,h^{-1}}=20\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$.

(a) $v=20-0.6(25)=5\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$. Multiply by $3.6$ to obtain $18\,\mathrm{km\,h^{-1}}$.

(b) $$s=\frac{20+5}{2}(25)=312.5\,\mathrm{m}.$$

Source: M1, Exercise 2A, Question 10; Source Files/2iec/Mechanics Chapt 01 SUVAT/M1 chapter 1 exercises.pdf, PDF page 9; Source Files/2iec/Mechanics Chapt 01 SUVAT/Solutions chapter 01.pdf, PDF page 10 (Exercise A, Question 10).

## 2A Q13

A particle moves from A through B to C with constant acceleration. Its speeds at A, B and C are $2\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$, $7\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$ and $11\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$. It takes $20\,\mathrm{s}$ from A to B. Find (a) its acceleration, (b) the time from B to C and (c) the distance AC.

- A. a) $0.25\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$; b) $36\,\mathrm{s}$; c) $234\,\mathrm{m}$
- B. a) $0.45\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$; b) $16\,\mathrm{s}$; c) $234\,\mathrm{m}$
- C. a) $0.25\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$; b) $16\,\mathrm{s}$; c) $144\,\mathrm{m}$
- D. a) $0.25\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$; b) $16\,\mathrm{s}$; c) $234\,\mathrm{m}$

Correct answer: D. a) $0.25\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$; b) $16\,\mathrm{s}$; c) $234\,\mathrm{m}$

Worked solution:

(a) $a=\frac{7-2}{20}=0.25\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$.

(b) $t_{BC}=\frac{11-7}{0.25}=16\,\mathrm{s}$.

(c) The total time is $20+16=36\,\mathrm{s}$.

$$AC=\frac{2+11}{2}(36)=234\,\mathrm{m}.$$

Source: M1, Exercise 2A, Question 13; Source Files/2iec/Mechanics Chapt 01 SUVAT/M1 chapter 1 exercises.pdf, PDF page 10; Source Files/2iec/Mechanics Chapt 01 SUVAT/Solutions chapter 01.pdf, PDF pages 13–14 (Exercise A, Question 13).

## 2A Q14

A particle moves along a straight line from A to B with constant acceleration $1.5\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$, then from B to C with a different constant acceleration. Its speeds at A and C are $1\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$ and $43\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$. The two stages take $12\,\mathrm{s}$ and $10\,\mathrm{s}$. Find (a) its speed at B, (b) its acceleration from B to C and (c) the distance AC.

- A. a) $18\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$; b) $2.5\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$; c) $430\,\mathrm{m}$
- B. a) $19\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$; b) $2.4\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$; c) $310\,\mathrm{m}$
- C. a) $19\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$; b) $2.4\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$; c) $430\,\mathrm{m}$
- D. a) $19\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$; b) $4.2\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$; c) $682\,\mathrm{m}$

Correct answer: C. a) $19\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$; b) $2.4\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$; c) $430\,\mathrm{m}$

Worked solution:

(a) $v_B=1+1.5(12)=19\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$.

(b) $a_{BC}=\frac{43-19}{10}=2.4\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$.

(c) Calculate each stage separately; the accelerations differ.

$$AB=\frac{1+19}{2}(12)=120\,\mathrm{m}.$$

$$BC=\frac{19+43}{2}(10)=310\,\mathrm{m}.$$

Therefore $AC=120+310=430\,\mathrm{m}$.

Source: M1, Exercise 2A, Question 14; Source Files/2iec/Mechanics Chapt 01 SUVAT/M1 chapter 1 exercises.pdf, PDF page 10; Source Files/2iec/Mechanics Chapt 01 SUVAT/Solutions chapter 01.pdf, PDF pages 15–16 (Exercise A, Question 14).

## 2A Q15

A cyclist accelerates uniformly from rest to $5\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$ in $20\,\mathrm{s}$ with acceleration $x$ m/s². She then decelerates uniformly to rest with deceleration $\frac{x}{2}$ m/s². Find (a) the value of $x$ and (b) the total distance travelled.

- A. a) $x=0.25$; b) $150\,\mathrm{m}$
- B. a) $x=0.25$; b) $100\,\mathrm{m}$
- C. a) $x=0.5$; b) $150\,\mathrm{m}$
- D. a) $x=0.25$; b) $75\,\mathrm{m}$

Correct answer: A. a) $x=0.25$; b) $150\,\mathrm{m}$

Worked solution:

(a) $5=0+20x$, so $x=0.25$.

(b) The first stage covers $\frac{0+5}{2}(20)=50\,\mathrm{m}$.

The second stage has acceleration $-0.125\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$, so its duration is $5/0.125=40\,\mathrm{s}$.

Its distance is $\frac{5+0}{2}(40)=100\,\mathrm{m}$. Total distance: $50+100=150\,\mathrm{m}$.

Source: M1, Exercise 2A, Question 15; Source Files/2iec/Mechanics Chapt 01 SUVAT/M1 chapter 1 exercises.pdf, PDF page 10; Source Files/2iec/Mechanics Chapt 01 SUVAT/Solutions chapter 01.pdf, PDF page 17 (Exercise A, Question 15).

## 2A Q16

A particle passes A, B and C in a straight line with constant acceleration at speeds $20\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$, $30\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$ and $45\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$. The times AB and BC are $t_1$ and $t_2$. Find (a) the ratio $\frac{t_1}{t_2}$ and (b) the distance AB if the total time AC is $50\,\mathrm{s}$.

- A. a) $\frac{t_1}{t_2}=\frac{3}{2}$; b) $750\,\mathrm{m}$
- B. a) $\frac{t_1}{t_2}=\frac{2}{3}$; b) $500\,\mathrm{m}$
- C. a) $\frac{t_1}{t_2}=\frac{2}{3}$; b) $1250\,\mathrm{m}$
- D. a) $\frac{t_1}{t_2}=\frac{1}{2}$; b) $500\,\mathrm{m}$

Correct answer: B. a) $\frac{t_1}{t_2}=\frac{2}{3}$; b) $500\,\mathrm{m}$

Worked solution:

(a) From $v=u+at$, $at_1=30-20=10$ and $at_2=45-30=15$. Divide to obtain $\frac{t_1}{t_2}=\frac{10}{15}=\frac{2}{3}$.

(b) Since $t_1+t_2=50$, $t_1=\frac{2}{5}(50)=20\,\mathrm{s}$.

$$AB=\frac{20+30}{2}(20)=500\,\mathrm{m}.$$

Source: M1, Exercise 2A, Question 16; Source Files/2iec/Mechanics Chapt 01 SUVAT/M1 chapter 1 exercises.pdf, PDF page 10; Source Files/2iec/Mechanics Chapt 01 SUVAT/Solutions chapter 01.pdf, PDF pages 18–19 (Exercise A, Question 16).
