## Scope and assumptions

- Exactly 10 four-option questions, with one correct answer per question.
- Five numerical bases: Questions 1, 2, 5, 6 and 8. Five variable bases: Questions 3, 4, 7, 9 and 10, using a or x.
- Four multiplication questions (add indices), three division questions (subtract indices), and three power-of-power questions (multiply indices).
- Keep the same base and express every answer as a single power, not an evaluated number.
- Positive integer indices only; zero, negative and fractional indices are outside this quiz.
- Variables are assumed non-zero, so all quotients are defined. Answers are algebraic identities, not evaluations at a particular variable value.
- Each law includes numerical and variable bases. Correct-answer positions vary: three A, two B, three C and two D.

## Laws used

- Same-base multiplication: $b^m\times b^n=b^{m+n}$.
- Same-base division: $b^m\div b^n=b^{m-n}$, with $b\neq0$.
- Power of a power: $(b^m)^n=b^{mn}$.
- Adding indices is a rule for multiplying powers; it does not mean adding the powered expressions.

## Student instructions

Write each answer as a single power with the same base. Do not evaluate numerical powers. Assume all variables are non-zero. Choose one answer for each question.

## 1. Multiplying powers — add the indices

Simplify $2^3\times2^4$. Write your answer as a single power.

- A. $2^{12}$
- B. $2^{7}$
- C. $2^{1}$
- D. $2^{6}$

Correct answer: B. $2^{7}$

Working:

When multiplying powers with the same base, keep the base and add the indices.

$2^3\times2^4=2^{3+4}=2^7$.

- B. $5^{1}$

Working:

Keep the base 5 and add the indices; do not multiply them.

## 3. Multiplying powers — add the indices

Simplify $a^4\times a^3$. Write your answer as a single power.

- A. $a^{7}$
- B. $a^{12}$
- C. $a^{1}$
- D. $a^{8}$

Correct answer: A. $a^{7}$

Working:

The base is a in both factors, so add the indices.

$a^4\times a^3=a^{4+3}=a^7$.

## 4. Multiplying powers — add the indices

Simplify $x^2\times x^6$. Write your answer as a single power.

- A. $x^{12}$
- B. $x^{4}$
- C. $x^{6}$
- D. $x^{8}$

Correct answer: D. $x^{8}$

Working:

Multiplication of powers with the same base means adding the indices.

$x^2\times x^6=x^{2+6}=x^8$.

## 5. Dividing powers — subtract the indices

Simplify $3^8\div3^3$. Write your answer as a single power.

- A. $3^{11}$
- B. $3^{5}$
- C. $3^{24}$
- D. $3^{4}$

Correct answer: B. $3^{5}$

Working:

When dividing powers with the same non-zero base, subtract the denominator's index from the numerator's index.

$3^8\div3^3=3^{8-3}=3^5$.

Source: Original.

## 6. Dividing powers — subtract the indices

Simplify $7^6\div7^2$. Write your answer as a single power.

- A. $7^{3}$
- B. $7^{8}$
- C. $7^{12}$
- D. $7^{4}$

Correct answer: D. $7^{4}$

Working:

Keep the base 7 and subtract the indices; do not divide them.

$7^6\div7^2=7^{6-2}=7^4$.

## 7. Dividing powers — subtract the indices

Simplify $a^9\div a^5$. Write your answer as a single power.

- A. $a^{4}$
- B. $a^{14}$
- C. $a^{45}$
- D. $a^{5}$

Correct answer: A. $a^{4}$

Working:

Since a is non-zero, division by its powers is defined. Subtract the second index from the first.

$a^9\div a^5=a^{9-5}=a^4$.

Source: Original.

## 8. Power of a power — multiply the indices

Simplify $(4^2)^3$. Write your answer as a single power.

- A. $4^{5}$
- B. $4^{8}$
- C. $4^{6}$
- D. $4^{9}$

Correct answer: C. $4^{6}$

Working:

When raising a power to another power, keep the base and multiply the indices.

$(4^2)^3=4^{2\times3}=4^6$.

Source: Original.

## 9. Power of a power — multiply the indices

Simplify $(x^3)^4$. Write your answer as a single power.

- A. $x^{12}$
- B. $x^{7}$
- C. $x^{9}$
- D. $x^{64}$

Correct answer: A. $x^{12}$

Working:

This is a power raised to a power, so multiply the indices instead of adding them.

$(x^3)^4=x^{3\times4}=x^{12}$.

Source: Original.

## 10. Power of a power — multiply the indices

Simplify $(a^5)^2$. Write your answer as a single power.

- A. $a^{7}$
- B. $a^{25}$
- C. $a^{10}$
- D. $a^{3}$

Correct answer: C. $a^{10}$

Working:

The brackets show a power of a power. Multiply the inner and outer indices.

$(a^5)^2=a^{5\times2}=a^{10}$.

Source: Original.
